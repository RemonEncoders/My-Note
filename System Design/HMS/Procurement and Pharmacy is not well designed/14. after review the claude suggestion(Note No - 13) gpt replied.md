# তারপরই আমি Design Freeze করতাম

Design Doc-এ আমি এই Principles লিখতাম।

### Principle 1

Drug is the System of Record.

---

### Principle 2

Product is a Procurement Projection.

---

### Principle 3

Synchronization is one-way only.

```text
Drug → Product
```

Never reverse.

---

### Principle 4

Clinical lifecycle and Procurement lifecycle are independent.

---

### Principle 5

Only Active Drugs can be Published.

---

### Principle 6

Published Products are immutable from SCM.

## তাহলে Claude-কে কী উত্তর দেবে?

আমি বলতাম:

> I agree with the simplified 3-state derived lifecycle and the guard that only `FormularyStatus == Active` drugs can be published. I don't think we need a separate approval state.
> 
> Before finalizing the design document, I'd like to resolve four remaining architectural decisions:
> 
> 1. Should `DrugPublishingService` depend directly on `ProductRepository`, or should it depend on an abstraction such as `IProductPublisher` to keep PHAR decoupled from SCM?
>     
> 2. What is the exact field mapping and synchronization policy from `Drug` to `Product`? Which fields are synchronized, and which must never be copied?
>     
> 3. What is the transaction boundary for publishing? How do we guarantee we never end up with a half-published drug if product creation fails?
>     
> 4. After a product is published, who owns its master data? Should SCM users be prevented from editing pharmacy-managed product fields to avoid divergence from the Drug master?
>     
> 
> Once these decisions are documented, I think the architecture will be complete enough to proceed with implementation.

**আমার মতে, এই চারটি প্রশ্নের উত্তর না দিয়ে implementation শুরু করা ঠিক হবে না।** এগুলো lifecycle-এর চেয়েও বেশি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ, কারণ এগুলো future maintenance, consistency এবং bounded context-এর integrity নির্ধারণ করবে।
