## Architecture Design Problem – Pharmacy Drug vs Inventory Product Integration

I need your help to solve an architecture problem, not just a coding problem. Please ignore implementation details initially and think from an enterprise ERP/Hospital Information System perspective (SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics, OpenMRS, etc.).

### Current Architecture

My system currently has three independent modules:

- **SCM (Supply Chain Management)** – Procurement
    
- **IMS (Inventory Management)** – Warehouse & Stock
    
- **PHAR (Pharmacy)** – Pharmacy Operations
    

Today, the procurement flow is:

```
Department
    ↓
Purchase Request
    ↓
IMS Review
    ↓
SCM Procurement
    ↓
Purchase Order
    ↓
Goods Receive
    ↓
Inventory Stock
    ↓
Transfer to Requesting Store
```

This flow already works correctly.

---

### Existing Data Model

The SCM and IMS modules are built around a **Product** master.

```
Product
    ↓
ItemStock
    ↓
Purchase Request
    ↓
Purchase Order
    ↓
Goods Receive
```

All procurement and inventory transactions reference **ProductId**.

---

### New Pharmacy Module

I am now designing a completely independent Pharmacy module.

The Pharmacy module has its own **Drug** master because it contains pharmacy-specific information that does not belong in the general Product master, such as:

- Generic
    
- Brand
    
- Strength
    
- Form
    
- Route
    
- TallMan Name
    
- High Alert
    
- Controlled Substance
    
- Cold Chain
    
- Formulary Status
    
- Clinical Review Date
    

The Drug entity is intentionally isolated from SCM and IMS because I want clear bounded contexts.

So Pharmacy becomes:

```
Drug
    ↓
Dispensing
```

Drug records are stored only in PHAR tables.

They are NOT stored in Product.

---

### The Business Problem

Now a Pharmacy Store wants to replenish stock.

The workflow should be:

```
Pharmacy Store
        ↓
Purchase Request
        ↓
IMS
        ↓
SCM Procurement
        ↓
Goods Receive
        ↓
Inventory Transfer
        ↓
Pharmacy Store
```

However, IMS Purchase Request currently allows selecting only **Product**.

The Pharmacy Store works only with **Drug**.

Therefore the Pharmacy cannot request inventory because IMS does not know anything about Drug.

Likewise, when IMS transfers stock back to Pharmacy, the transfer mechanism expects Product-based inventory.

---

### Design Constraints

I do NOT want the following simplistic solutions unless they are truly industry best practice:

- Duplicating every Drug as a Product.
    
- Merging Drug into Product.
    
- Making Product inherit Drug or Drug inherit Product.
    
- Tight coupling between PHAR and SCM.
    

Those approaches seem to create synchronization problems and violate bounded contexts.

---

### What I Need

From an enterprise software architecture perspective:

1. How do large ERP/HIS systems solve this problem?
    
2. What is the correct domain model?
    
3. Should there be a shared inventory identity, catalog, item abstraction, or another pattern?
    
4. How can Pharmacy remain an independent module while still participating in procurement and inventory workflows?
    
5. What would you recommend if the system needs to scale to multiple hospitals and future modules?
    

Please focus on architecture, domain modeling, and long-term maintainability rather than the easiest implementation.
